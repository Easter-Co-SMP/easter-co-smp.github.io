# Easter Company launcher 1.0.22

Two corners of the interface that were too small for their own contents, both on a small window.

- **The drawing palette fits above the bar.** On a phone-sized window the palette's panel ran under the launcher's own progress bar, so Colour and Stroke sat below the edge of the screen — out of reach before a single message was on screen, and worse once one was. The panel is measured against the first thing under it, the chart's footer or the bar, and scrolls inside the room that is left, so every tool and both colour controls can be reached. A desktop window is untouched.
- **Draw follows the window's width.** Where the palette sits was decided when the map loaded: a window that started narrow and was then widened left the palette over the edge of the map key, where a click opened Draw instead of the key, and a window that started wide put it 18 pixels too far right after narrowing. It now reads its position from the width it is at, in both directions, on the website and in the launcher.
- **The wrong-account message is readable everywhere.** On a phone width the three-line conflict refusal now scrolls into view instead of being cut off at the window's edge — its last line, the one that says what to do, used to end under the window's own border. At the launcher's 800x600 minimum the three lines fit at once and the account window no longer shows a scrollbar over space it cannot show. Sign in and Forgot your password stay below the refusal at that width, in the order a refusal is read.

Everything from 1.0.21 and before is unchanged: one name for signing in, the identity-conflict stop that explains itself, a failure notice that reserves its own room on every page, sign-in messages with an action per cause and no provider codes, the redacted `signin.log` and `install.log`, the verified-email picker and the twelve wiki guides.

Validation covers Node and Python unit tests, the browser suite against a local preview, the drawing suite and Electron journeys through the real main process, plus new assertions for both fixes at 1440, 800 and 390 — each with a negative control, so the tests fail the way the defects failed. A fourth round of independent frontend review ran against this tree before publishing. No real Microsoft approval was exercised here; the sign-in chain is proven against injected Microsoft, Xbox Live and Minecraft answers and the provider endpoints were probed with unauthenticated requests only. If something still refuses, `signin.log` names the sign-in step and `install.log` names the install failure.
