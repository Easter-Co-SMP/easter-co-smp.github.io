Equipment & enchanting

XP is not the usual progression gate. Check the changed enchanting table, anvil support, smithing upgrades and built-in effects before investing in gear.

XP and the enchanting table

XP persists through ordinary death, and the pack’s automatic XP removal is disabled. The enchanting table’s offer list is empty, so it is not the usual route to rolling enchantments.

Opening an anvil triggers support that raises a player below 100 levels to 100 without reducing a player already above it. This makes the level cost a different consideration from vanilla. Anvil compatibility, materials and other item rules still apply: having enough levels does not guarantee every combination is valid.

Mending has not been removed by the XP patch. Do not throw away useful equipment on the assumption that XP and all enchantment effects have disappeared.

Smithing upgrades

Custom smithing recipes add equipment paths beyond the familiar vanilla tier list. Use the displayed ingredient names; some custom items reuse an underlying vanilla type with a different name and purpose.

Steel Alloy is the pack’s displayed name for this ingredient. Judge the result by its actual tooltip and purpose rather than assuming a material’s position from a vanilla tier chart. The Steel Sword changes attack behavior, including knockback and speed; it is not just a renamed iron sword.

Built-in effects and repair

Some equipment carries custom or intrinsic enchantments. These belong to the item’s design and can be applied when it enters your inventory; an empty enchanting-table offer list does not mean a custom item has no effects.

Read the resulting item’s tooltip after obtaining or upgrading it. Check the anvil’s output and required materials before completing a repair or combination. Use the item’s own stats instead of a general promise that all repair is free or that every custom effect can be transferred.

Avoid these vanilla assumptions

Plan upgrades around the actual recipe and resulting item. These are the common progression traps:

  • Do not build an enchanting setup expecting a normal offer list.
  • Do not confuse an item’s underlying vanilla type with its displayed custom material or equipment role.
  • Do not assume every special item or built-in effect has an ordinary enchanted-book equivalent.
  • Do not depend on armour alone for recovery: natural health regeneration is disabled.
