Wildlife & pets

Name tags, ownership and the island’s register have different effects. Know which animals are protected by law and which have a custom return rule.

Named, owned and registered

A name is not ownership, and ownership is not a respawn guarantee. Naming a creature can give it protection under Navy law; only the island’s specific lifecycle rules decide whether it returns after death.

For crime pricing, a tameable creature counts as a pet even when it has not yet been tamed. Named non-tameable livestock use a different price tier. Island villagers receive names automatically and are protected people.

A name tag does not grant immortality

Do not expect a player-named wolf, cat or other pet to return just because it has a name.

Protection by law

In Navy-controlled Overworld territory, harming or killing someone else’s named creature is an offence. Untagged wild animals are not protected by that name-tag rule. Recognized ownership exempts your own animal; the law also has an exception for a pirate owner’s assets.

An initial hit is a base price: repeated non-flat offences and an active Criminal mark can change the charge. Being lawfully able to harm something does not make it a good idea or grant a respawn.

Animals that return

The island maintains selected named livestock and registered animals. Their return behavior is tied to the island and its records, not to every animal of the same species in the world.

  • The protected island species include sheep, chickens, pigs and trader llamas; species-specific and registered-name rules narrow how they are managed.
  • The bull and the two registered horses are specific animals, not a rule protecting or respawning every cow or horse.
  • The island’s ordinary cats are replenished after local extinction rather than each cat being an individually protected respawn.
  • These clocks run with the relevant game/world conditions. Do not substitute a real-world return-time promise for an in-game day.

The two registered horses

Clyde and Bonnie are the island’s two registered horses, with their own owners, coats and return records. Their rules do not apply to a newly tamed horse merely given the same name.

Each has its own death-return cycle. A registered horse separated from its online owner by at least 32 chunks in the same dimension is brought home under the owner-separation rule. An offline owner or an owner in another dimension is not treated as the same separation case.

Treat these as specific registered animals. Renaming another horse does not transfer their identity, ownership or return behavior.
